Part 2

Correlation Analysis

Correlation measures the strength and direction of the relationship between two variables. It tells us whether changes in one variable are associated with changes in another.

Pearson’s r (linear)

r = Σ(xᵢ−x̄)(yᵢ−ȳ) / √(Σ(xᵢ−x̄)² Σ(yᵢ−ȳ)²)

Measures linear relationships between two continuous variables.

Spearman’s ρ (monotonic)

ρ = 1 − 6Σdᵢ² / (n(n²−1))

Works on ranks, so it captures monotonic (not necessarily linear) relationships and resists outliers.

Signal correlations

For time series, correlation is computed as a function of the lag τ. Cross-correlation compares two different signals; auto-correlation compares a signal with a shifted copy of itself.

Cross-correlation: find the delay

Rxy(τ) = Σ x(t) y(t+τ)

A temperature sensor responds to a vibration event after an unknown delay. Slide τ until the shifted vibration (blue) lines up with temperature (orange).

Auto-correlation: detect periodicity

Rxx(τ) = Σ x(t) x(t+τ)

A vibration signal from a rotating machine. Peaks in the ACF reveal the cycle length, even when noise hides it in the raw signal.

Part 4

What is Causality?

Causality is the study of cause and effect: whether and how one thing (the treatment) produces a change in another (the outcome). A variable X is a cause of Y if Y “listens” to X, meaning Y changes its value in response to X.

Industry

Regular maintenance → fewer machine breakdowns

Healthcare

Vaccination → lower infection rates

Environment

More CO₂ emissions → higher global temperatures

Three building blocks of causal graphs

Part 5

Root Cause Analysis (RCA)

RCA identifies the origin of a problem rather than its symptoms. In connected systems, one faulty component makes everything downstream look faulty too.

Classical

5 Whys and fishbone

Expert-led questioning and cause categories (machine, method, material, people, environment). Fast and intuitive, but depends on who is in the room.

Associational

Rank the biggest deviations

Flag the metrics that moved the most. Finds symptoms quickly, but a component may look abnormal only because it inherited a delay.

Causal

Graph + fitted mechanisms

Model how each component depends on its parents. A root cause is a node whose own behavior is abnormal given its inputs.
